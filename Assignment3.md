3. Project constraints essay. A short essay of 10 to 12 sentences total addressing 3 to 4 of the eight topical areas below. For each area, say whether it factors into your project and how it shapes the solutions that are viable. Ground every claim in a fact about your project: a budget number, a named policy, a named user group. Name at least one concrete trade-off where satisfying one constraint puts pressure on another. Save it as Constraints_Essay.md with a header naming the project and all team members, use the topical-area names as your headings, adding the syllabus category in parentheses where it differs (regulatory is legal, sustainability is environmental, manufacturability is economic and professional), and submit a PDF.
Topical area

Economic
Financial limitations? Freeware or purchases? Personal or company funds? Restricted to UC facilities? Contribution to economic development?

Professional
Impact on professional development or public reputation? Is specialized expertise needed?

Ethical
Direct or indirect moral impact? What precautions keep it positive or neutral?

Legal
Legal issues? Disputed intellectual property?

Security
Viruses, eavesdropping, privacy?

Social
Public service, non-profit use, societal benefit, quality of life?

Environmental
Limits on where or how it is deployed? Pollution or harmful side effects? Does it reduce the impact of existing solutions?

Diversity and Cultural
Language, lifestyle, local customs? Promoting diverse use?
 

Suggestions
Shortlist two or three faculty whose work touches the technical areas in your topic statement, and send one short, specific email: a paragraph on the problem, why their expertise fits, and two or three meeting times.
Count the sentences. Ten to twelve means ten to twelve, and three areas done well beat all eight labeled.
Cut definitions. Explaining what an economic constraint is uses a sentence you needed for your own budget.
Look for two areas that pull in different directions, such as security against cost, and write one sentence naming the tension and the balance you chose.
A fit sentence is about fit. "Dr. X is a great professor" is not a rationale; name the expertise and the project part it serves.
